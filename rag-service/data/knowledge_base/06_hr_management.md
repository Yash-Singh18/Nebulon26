# HR and Team Management for Small Businesses

## Hiring the Right People

Hiring is one of the most impactful decisions for a small business. A great hire multiplies your capacity; a poor hire drains your time, money, and team morale.

### Writing Effective Job Descriptions

Write job descriptions that attract the right candidates. Include the specific responsibilities and expected outcomes, required skills versus nice-to-have skills, compensation range to filter for realistic expectations, company culture and values, and growth opportunities within the role.

Avoid vague descriptions like looking for a rockstar or wearing many hats. Specific descriptions attract specific, qualified candidates.

### The Hiring Process

Screen resumes for must-have qualifications. Conduct a brief phone screen of 15-20 minutes to assess communication and basic fit. Then hold structured interviews where every candidate gets the same questions.

Use behavioral interview questions: tell me about a time when you handled a difficult customer, or describe a situation where you had to meet a tight deadline. Past behavior predicts future performance.

Include a practical assessment or work sample test relevant to the role. For developers, a coding challenge. For marketers, a sample campaign plan. For salespeople, a mock pitch.

Check references. Ask specific questions: what was the candidate's biggest strength, what area needed improvement, and would you rehire them.

### Avoiding Common Hiring Mistakes

Do not hire based on gut feeling alone. Use structured evaluation criteria and score each candidate. Do not rush the process due to desperation. A vacant position is better than a wrong hire.

Hire for attitude and cultural fit. Skills can be developed; attitude, integrity, and work ethic are deeply ingrained.

## Onboarding New Employees

The first 90 days determine whether a new hire succeeds or fails. Create a structured onboarding plan.

Week 1: orientation including company history, values, tools, and team introductions. Assign a buddy or mentor for questions.

Month 1: structured training on their specific role. Set clear 30-day goals. Schedule daily 15-minute check-ins.

Month 2-3: increase responsibility gradually. Set 60 and 90-day milestones. Conduct a formal 90-day review.

## Employee Retention

Replacing an employee costs 50-200% of their annual salary when you factor in recruiting, training, and lost productivity. Retention should be a top priority.

### Compensation and Benefits

Pay competitively. Research market rates using sites like Glassdoor, Payscale, or industry surveys. You do not have to be the highest payer, but being significantly below market guarantees turnover.

For small businesses that cannot match corporate salaries, offer non-monetary benefits: flexible schedules, remote work options, professional development budget, extra PTO, or equity and profit sharing.

### Career Development

Employees leave when they see no growth path. Create development plans with each team member. Invest in training, conferences, and certifications. Promote from within whenever possible.

Regular one-on-one meetings are essential. Meet with each direct report weekly or biweekly to discuss progress, challenges, and career goals. This is the single most effective retention tool.

### Culture and Engagement

Build a culture of recognition. Acknowledge good work publicly and specifically. Studies show that employees who feel recognized are 63% more likely to stay.

Foster transparency. Share business goals, challenges, and financial performance with your team. When employees understand the bigger picture, they are more engaged and make better decisions.

## Performance Management

### Setting Expectations

Set SMART goals: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. Every employee should know exactly what success looks like in their role.

### Giving Feedback

Provide feedback regularly, not just during annual reviews. Use the SBI model: describe the Situation, the Behavior you observed, and the Impact it had.

Deliver constructive feedback privately and promptly. Do not stockpile feedback for months. Address issues within 48 hours while context is fresh.

### Handling Underperformance

Document performance issues in writing. Put underperforming employees on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) with specific, measurable goals and a clear timeline, typically 30-60 days.

If improvement does not happen despite support and clear expectations, move to termination. Keeping underperformers drags down the entire team.

## Payroll and Compliance

### Payroll Basics

Choose between running payroll yourself and using a payroll service like Gusto, ADP, or Paychex. Payroll services handle tax calculations, withholdings, filings, and direct deposits for a monthly fee.

Pay employees on a consistent schedule: weekly, biweekly, or semi-monthly. Never miss a payroll date.

### Employee Classification

Correctly classify workers as W-2 employees or 1099 independent contractors. The key test is control: if you control when, where, and how someone works, they are likely an employee.

Misclassification can result in back taxes, penalties, and legal liability. When in doubt, classify as an employee.

## Building a Small Team Culture

For teams of 2-15 people, culture is set directly by the owner's behavior. Model the work ethic, communication style, and values you want to see.

Create simple rituals: Monday team standups, Friday wins celebrations, monthly team lunches, or quarterly planning sessions. These build cohesion and shared purpose.

Address conflicts immediately. In small teams, unresolved tension affects everyone. Facilitate direct conversations between conflicting parties and mediate if needed.
