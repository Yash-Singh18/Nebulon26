# Tax Planning and Compliance for Small Businesses

## Understanding Business Tax Obligations

Every business must understand its tax obligations. These vary based on business structure, location, and revenue. The key categories are income tax, self-employment tax, payroll tax, and sales tax.

### Tax Filing Deadlines

For sole proprietors and single-member LLCs, taxes are due April 15. Partnerships and S-corporations file by March 15. Corporations file by April 15. Always set calendar reminders 30 days before deadlines.

Estimated quarterly tax payments are due April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the following year. Pay at least 90% of your current year liability or 100% of last year's liability to avoid penalties.

## Common Tax Deductions for Small Businesses

### Home Office Deduction

If you use a dedicated space in your home exclusively for business, you can deduct a portion of your rent or mortgage, utilities, insurance, and maintenance. The simplified method allows $5 per square foot up to 300 square feet. The regular method calculates the actual percentage of your home used for business.

### Vehicle and Travel Expenses

Track business mileage using an app. The standard mileage rate is updated annually. Alternatively, deduct actual vehicle expenses proportional to business use. Keep a detailed mileage log with dates, destinations, and business purpose.

Business travel including airfare, hotels, and 50% of meals is deductible when the primary purpose of the trip is business-related.

### Equipment and Depreciation

Section 179 allows small businesses to deduct the full purchase price of qualifying equipment in the year of purchase, up to the annual limit. This includes computers, furniture, vehicles, and software.

Bonus depreciation allows 100% first-year deduction for new and used equipment. This is especially valuable for capital-intensive businesses.

### Other Key Deductions

Professional services: legal fees, accounting fees, and consulting expenses are fully deductible.
Insurance premiums: business insurance, health insurance for self-employed individuals, and workers compensation.
Marketing and advertising: all marketing expenses including digital ads, print materials, and website costs.
Education and training: courses, certifications, and conferences that improve business skills.
Software and subscriptions: SaaS tools, cloud services, and professional memberships.
Interest on business loans: fully deductible as a business expense.

## Tax-Efficient Business Structures

### Sole Proprietorship

Simplest structure. Business income flows to your personal return. You pay income tax plus self-employment tax (15.3%) on all net earnings. No corporate-level tax.

### LLC (Limited Liability Company)

By default taxed as a sole proprietorship or partnership. Provides liability protection without changing your tax situation. Can elect S-corp taxation to potentially reduce self-employment tax.

### S-Corporation

Profits pass through to personal returns, but only salary is subject to self-employment tax. Distributions are not subject to self-employment tax. You must pay yourself a reasonable salary. Potential savings if your business earns significantly more than a reasonable salary.

### C-Corporation

Taxed at the corporate rate (currently 21% in the US). Dividends to shareholders are taxed again at personal rates (double taxation). Best for businesses planning to retain significant earnings or seeking venture capital.

## Sales Tax Compliance

If you sell products or taxable services, you must collect and remit sales tax. After the Wayfair decision, you may have nexus (obligation to collect) in states where you have significant sales, even without physical presence.

Use sales tax automation software like TaxJar or Avalara to calculate, collect, and remit taxes across multiple jurisdictions.

## Record Keeping for Tax Purposes

Maintain organized records for at least 7 years. Categories to track: income received, expenses paid, assets purchased, payroll records, and bank statements.

Use cloud-based accounting software that automatically categorizes transactions and generates tax-ready reports. This reduces preparation time and minimizes errors.

## Working With Tax Professionals

Hire a CPA or tax advisor if your business has employees, multiple revenue streams, or complex deductions. A good tax professional saves more in deductions and penalty avoidance than they cost.

Meet with your tax advisor quarterly, not just at tax time. Year-round tax planning helps you make strategic decisions about equipment purchases, retirement contributions, and business structure changes before the year ends.

## Tax Planning Calendar

January: Review previous year, gather documents, and file W-2s and 1099s.
April: File annual returns or extensions. Make Q1 estimated payment.
June: Mid-year tax review. Make Q2 estimated payment.
September: Make Q3 estimated payment. Consider year-end tax strategies.
December: Make final year-end purchases for deductions. Review retirement contributions.
