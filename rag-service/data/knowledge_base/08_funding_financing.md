# Funding and Financing Options for Small Businesses

## Bootstrapping

Bootstrapping means funding your business from personal savings and revenue. This is the most common funding method for small businesses and retains 100% ownership.

### Bootstrapping Strategies

Start lean with a minimum viable product. Launch with the smallest possible investment that still delivers value. Validate demand before investing heavily in inventory, equipment, or marketing.

Use pre-sales and deposits to fund production. Sell before you build. If customers are willing to pay upfront, you have market validation and working capital simultaneously.

Reinvest profits aggressively in the early years. Pay yourself a modest salary and put the rest back into growth. This compounds quickly if your business has healthy margins.

Reduce personal expenses to extend your runway. The lower your personal burn rate, the more time you have to make the business work.

## Business Loans

### Traditional Bank Loans

Banks offer the lowest interest rates but have strict requirements: strong personal credit score (680+), 2+ years of business history, collateral, and detailed business plans with financial projections.

Prepare a loan package including your business plan, 3 years of tax returns, financial statements, bank statements, and a description of how you will use the funds.

### SBA Loans

SBA (Small Business Administration) loans are partially guaranteed by the government, making banks more willing to lend. The most common is the SBA 7(a) loan for up to $5 million with terms up to 25 years.

SBA loans have competitive interest rates and longer repayment terms than conventional loans. The application process takes 2-3 months, so plan ahead.

### Online Lenders

Online lenders like Kabbage, OnDeck, and Fundbox offer faster approval (often within 24-48 hours) with less stringent requirements. However, interest rates are higher, often 10-40% APR.

Use online lenders for short-term needs like bridging a cash flow gap or seizing a time-sensitive opportunity. For long-term financing, pursue traditional loans.

### Lines of Credit

A business line of credit provides flexible access to funds. You draw what you need and pay interest only on the borrowed amount. Ideal for managing cash flow fluctuations, seasonal inventory purchases, or unexpected expenses.

Apply for a line of credit before you need it. Banks are more willing to extend credit when your business is healthy.

## Equity Financing

### Angel Investors

Angel investors are wealthy individuals who invest their own money in early-stage businesses, typically $25,000 to $500,000. They often provide mentorship and industry connections in addition to capital.

To attract angels: have a validated product, demonstrate traction (revenue or strong user growth), show a large addressable market, and present a clear path to profitability or exit.

Find angel investors through local angel groups, startup events, AngelList, and personal network referrals.

### Venture Capital

Venture capital firms invest larger amounts ($1M+) in businesses with high growth potential. In exchange, they take equity and often board seats.

VC is only appropriate for businesses aiming for rapid, massive scale. Most small businesses are not suitable for venture capital and should not pursue it.

If VC is right for you: prepare a compelling pitch deck, demonstrate product-market fit, show a scalable business model, and present a clear plan for 10x or greater return.

### Crowdfunding

Rewards-based crowdfunding on platforms like Kickstarter or Indiegogo lets you pre-sell products and validate demand simultaneously. Success requires a compelling campaign with professional video, clear rewards tiers, and an active marketing plan.

Equity crowdfunding platforms like Wefunder and Republic allow you to sell small stakes to many investors. This has become increasingly popular for small businesses that do not fit the VC model.

## Grants and Alternative Funding

### Small Business Grants

Government grants are free money that does not need to be repaid. However, they are competitive and often restricted to specific industries, demographics, or purposes.

Check Grants.gov, your state economic development agency, and SBA resources for available grants. Local economic development organizations often have grants for businesses in their communities.

### Microloans

Microloans of $500 to $50,000 from nonprofit lenders serve entrepreneurs who may not qualify for traditional bank loans. The SBA Microloan program provides up to $50,000 with terms up to 6 years.

Microloans often come with business training and mentorship, making them especially valuable for first-time business owners.

### Revenue-Based Financing

Revenue-based financing provides a lump sum in exchange for a percentage of future monthly revenue until a fixed amount is repaid. There is no fixed repayment schedule, and payments fluctuate with your revenue.

This is good for businesses with strong, consistent revenue but limited collateral or credit history.

## Financial Planning for Growth

### Creating Financial Projections

Build 3-year financial projections including income statements, cash flow statements, and balance sheets. Use conservative revenue estimates and realistic expense assumptions.

Model different scenarios: best case, base case, and worst case. This helps you understand how much funding you need and when you will break even.

### Understanding Your Funding Needs

Calculate your total capital requirements: startup costs, operating expenses until break-even, and a buffer of 20-30% for unexpected expenses.

Determine the right funding mix. Generally, use debt for tangible assets with predictable returns and equity for growth with uncertain timelines.

### Choosing the Right Funding Source

Consider these factors: how much do you need, how fast do you need it, what can you afford in repayment or equity dilution, what are the requirements for approval, and how much control are you willing to share.

Do not take more money than you need. More capital means more dilution or more debt. But do not take too little — running out of money before hitting milestones is worse than a slightly higher dilution.

## Preparing for Fundraising

Regardless of funding source, organize your finances: clean up your books, separate personal and business finances, resolve any tax issues, and prepare detailed financial statements. Investors and lenders need to trust your financial management before they trust you with their money.
